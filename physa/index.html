M(q) — the memristance curve

q runs −1 to 1. Math is in scope, so sin, exp, abs and PI all work. Values are clamped to at least 20 Ω so the current stays finite. Presets set the curve and every control below it.

Wear

μ sets how far a given current moves the charge, and so how much of the curve one cycle sweeps. τ pulls the charge back toward zero when nothing is driving it: short τ reaches equilibrium at once and holds a steady timbre, long τ keeps wearing while a note is held and carries the state into the next note.

Senescence

Playing abrades the curve. Where the charge dwells, M(q) blends toward its own mean, so the played stretch loses its shape. The dashed line on the display is the equation you wrote; the solid line is what is left of it. The half-life is quoted at amplitude 1 — louder wears faster. Fresh substrate clears it; editing the equation does not.

Colony

Several elements across the same drive, sharing one substrate. Spread gives them different rates μ, so they sit at different points on the curve at once. Reinforcement is Tero's tube law: an element carrying more current thickens and carries more still, an idle one thins back. At 0 the elements are fixed and sum.

Mould

A capacitance across the element. It carries current and holds no state, so it opens the loop into an ellipse that misses the origin and adds nothing above the fundamental. Raise it and memory climbs while harmonic stays at zero. Its share grows with pitch, which is the inverse of how the element behaves.

M(q) across the charge domain. Dashed is the equation as written, solid is the substrate after wear, and the strip along the bottom is how far each part of the domain has worn. Each marker is one element of the colony, sized by its tube thickness. Playing moves them; releasing lets τ carry them back.

Current against drive voltage over one cycle, taken before the gain stage. A memristance that varies with charge opens the line into a loop; a constant M(q) collapses it to a straight line. Memory is the share of Fourier energy in coefficients no memoryless device can produce. Harmonic is the share above the fundamental. Pinch is |I| at the voltage zero-crossings, where 0% means the loop passes through the origin — Chua's criterion for memristance, necessary and disputed as sufficient.

Output current. Low notes wear the element further within a cycle and come out dirtier: at the default settings, harmonic content above the fundamental runs 4.5% at 110 Hz, 0.76% at 220, 0.18% at 440 and 0.04% at 880 — about a quarter per octave. No keyfollow rule is written anywhere; it falls out of the charge integrating. Below 110 Hz the figure climbs steeply and turns sensitive to μ: at 55 Hz it is 30% at the default rate and 1.6% at a third of it, so treat that end as a setting rather than a property. Amplitude enters the same way, 0.2% at 0.25 rising to 18% at 2.

Keyboard row a w s e d f t g y h u j k also plays. With a long τ, holding a note dulls it as the charge settles — at τ = 3 s, 4.9% down to 3.5% harmonic content over 2.2 s, and further with relaxation off. At τ = 20 ms it reaches equilibrium at once and holds 5.4% flat instead.

Drive

The current is v/M(q), so output level tracks 1/M and swings as widely as the curve — 80:1 on the HP preset. Auto level is a slow (80 ms) gain that flattens the swing without touching the waveform inside a cycle, so timbre and keyfollow survive it. Turn it off to hear the raw current.

The name

Physa is Greek for bellows, and it is the root of Physarum, the slime mould named for its sporangia. That organism was reported as a biological memristor in 2015 (Gale, de Lacy Costello and Adamatzky), and roughly a decade of computer music was built on it. In 2025 Schmidt, Seyfried, Reutina, Seskir and Miranda characterised multiple specimens and found no significant memristance: elliptical I–V curves produced by the organism's own capacitance, which they reproduced with resistors and capacitors alone (MRS Advances 10(14): 1710–1716). Miranda had composed the music. The Mould control puts that measurement on a dial, so the false positive is audible and visible beside the ideal element it was mistaken for.

Why this is not a simulation of a device

Chua deduced the memristor in 1971 from the symmetry of v, i, q, φ — it existed as a gap in a table before it existed as anything else. Whether matter can realise the ideal element is disputed: Vongehr and Meng argue it is likely impossible (Sci. Rep. 5:11657), and Pershin and Di Ventra's charge-only test has disqualified claimed devices. Those results constrain hardware. Here the element is exactly its definition, and M(q) can be any curve at all — periodic in charge, discontinuous, whatever matter would refuse.

What it claims

The claim is about specification, not about reachable timbres — any single instant of this output is reachable by some waveshaper. One equation and two constants yield a nonlinearity that keyfollows, tracks level, and carries history across notes, none of which was written as a rule. Senescence extends that to a third timescale: the curve itself is a state variable, and an hour of playing leaves an element that no longer matches its own definition.
